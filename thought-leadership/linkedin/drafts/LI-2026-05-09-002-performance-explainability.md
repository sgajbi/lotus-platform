Performance analytics must be explainable before it is impressive.

In portfolio platforms, a beautiful return chart is not enough. The user eventually asks the harder
questions:

What period is this?
Which cash flows were included?
Which benchmark is used?
Is this money-weighted or time-weighted?
Why does this differ from another report?
What is provisional, stale, missing, or restated?

If the platform cannot answer those questions, the visual becomes a liability.

This is why I think methodology, lineage, and state handling should be designed as product features,
not back-office documentation. The calculation is only one part of the experience. The explanation
around the calculation is what allows portfolio managers, advisors, risk teams, and clients to trust
it.

Good analytics products do not hide complexity. They structure it so the right user can understand
the right level of detail at the right time.

That is often the difference between a dashboard people admire and a platform people actually use.
