A mandate is not just a label on a portfolio.

In a good wealth platform, a mandate behaves more like an operating contract.

It connects investment intent to the practical controls around the portfolio:

the model or strategy,
the allowed instruments,
the policy constraints,
the booking center,
the reference currency,
the portfolio manager,
the exception process,
the evidence needed for review.

When these elements are disconnected, discretionary portfolio management becomes harder to operate.
Users can see a portfolio, but they cannot easily understand whether it is aligned, drifting,
blocked, or ready for action.

That is why mandate design should not be treated as static reference data. It belongs in the workflow
and in the operating model.

The platform should help answer: what is this portfolio meant to be, what governs it, what changed,
what needs attention, and who owns the next decision?

That is where mandate technology becomes useful beyond administration. It becomes a control surface
for portfolio management.
