In wealth platforms, source-data ownership is not a back-office detail.

It is one of the conditions for trust.

A portfolio view may combine holdings, prices, cash flows, benchmarks, risk measures,
performance returns, restrictions, and mandate context. Each of those facts can have a different
owner, refresh cycle, methodology, and supportability state.

Good platforms make that ownership visible in the design.

They do not ask every downstream screen, report, or workflow to reinterpret the same data in a
slightly different way. They define who owns the fact, how it is produced, what methodology applies,
when it is stale, when it is partial, and what users should see when it cannot be trusted.

That discipline matters because portfolio analytics is rarely challenged only on the final number.
It is challenged on the path to the number.

Where did it come from?
Why did it change?
Is it complete?
Can it be explained in a review?

For wealth technology, source-data ownership is not just governance. It is product design.
