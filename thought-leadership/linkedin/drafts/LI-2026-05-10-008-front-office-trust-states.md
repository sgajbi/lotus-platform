Front-office users do not only need more data.

They need to know whether the data is usable.

In a wealth platform, a portfolio view can be correct in one section, incomplete in another, stale
in a third, and blocked by missing source data somewhere else. If the interface treats all of those
states the same, the user has to do the interpretation manually.

That is where adoption becomes harder than it needs to be.

Good platforms make trust states part of the product language. They distinguish ready, partial,
stale, unavailable, and blocked information clearly enough that a portfolio manager, advisor,
operations user, or support team can act with the right level of confidence.

This is not just a UI pattern. It is an operating control.

It helps users understand when they can proceed, when they should review, when they need support,
and when the system should stop rather than imply certainty.

Front-office adoption improves when platforms are honest about what they know, what they do not
know, and what should happen next.
