One useful delivery habit is keeping a clear work-to-be-done ledger after a major design or roadmap
item is closed.

Not a backlog in disguise. Not a parking lot for vague ideas.

A real WTBD ledger should say what was completed, what was deliberately left out, who owns the next
piece, what evidence is needed, and what must not be claimed yet.

That distinction matters in banking platforms because large capabilities rarely become complete in
one clean step. A workflow may have the core service, but not the front-office experience. It may
have the calculation, but not the report. It may have the happy path, but not the degraded-state
proof. It may have a useful first release, but not every source-data owner or downstream control.

Without a disciplined ledger, teams can accidentally turn partial delivery into product mythology.

With one, the conversation becomes sharper.

What is genuinely supported?
What is proven?
What remains a source-owner responsibility?
What needs evidence before it becomes part of the product story?

Good platforms need this kind of operational memory.

It keeps delivery honest without slowing it down.
