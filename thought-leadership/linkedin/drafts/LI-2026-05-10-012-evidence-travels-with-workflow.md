One practical lesson from building banking workflows is that evidence cannot be an afterthought.

It has to travel with the workflow.

In a portfolio process, the decision path may move from mandate context, source data, analytics,
construction, review, approval, reporting, and outcome assessment. If each step produces its own
isolated explanation, the platform may look complete but still be hard to trust.

Good platforms preserve the thread.

Which source facts were used?
Which constraints mattered?
Which supportability state was present at the time?
Which version of the analysis was approved?
Which report or outcome later referred back to it?

This is where functional and non-functional design meet. The user sees a business workflow, but the
platform also needs stable identity, lineage, auditability, access control, exception handling, and
clear documentation underneath.

That does not make the experience heavier. Done well, it makes the experience calmer.

Users do not have to hunt for the story behind the result.

The story is already attached to the work.
