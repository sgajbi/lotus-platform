Demo data is not a side detail in wealth platforms.

It shapes what the product can honestly prove.

If the data is too thin, the screen may look clean while the real workflow remains untested. If the
data is inconsistent, users may spend the demo questioning the numbers instead of understanding the
capability. If the data is disconnected from the APIs, screenshots become presentation material
rather than evidence.

Canonical data helps solve this.

A governed portfolio, known as-of date, expected supportability thresholds, realistic analytics,
and repeatable validation scripts create a stable test surface. Teams can ask sharper questions:

Does this portfolio appear consistently across screens?
Do the calculations and status states align?
Do review actions preserve the right lineage?
Can the result be reproduced tomorrow?

That is useful for engineering, but it is also useful for product conversations.

Good canonical data keeps demos honest. It stops teams from proving only the easiest path and
forces the platform to show whether the full workflow is coherent, supported, and explainable.
