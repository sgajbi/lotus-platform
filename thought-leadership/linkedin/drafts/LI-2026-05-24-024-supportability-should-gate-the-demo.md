A demo can show that a product is attractive.

It should also show that the product is supportable.

For internal banking platforms, this matters because the people who operate, support, and extend the system need more than a polished path. They need to know what happens when data is missing, a dependency is degraded, or a workflow is only partially supported.

That is why supportability should gate the demo evidence.

Before screenshots are treated as proof, the stack should validate the APIs, panel states, portfolio context, errors, logs, metrics, and documented capability boundaries.

If something is not ready, the system should say so in business language. If a feature depends on an external source or a future source-owner product, that boundary should be visible.

This is not about making demos slower.

It is about making demos more honest.

A strong platform can demonstrate a workflow and explain the operational evidence behind it. That gives product teams, engineers, business stakeholders, and support teams a shared view of what is real.

Pretty screens help adoption. Supportable screens earn trust.
