Candidate discovery sounds like a simple product problem.

Find the portfolios. Build the workflow. Let the user act.

In private banking platforms, it is rarely that simple.

A portfolio can belong to a mandate, model, booking center, relationship structure, operating policy, and source-data lifecycle. If the workflow layer quietly discovers candidates on its own, it can blur ownership very quickly.

The better pattern is to make candidate discovery a source-owned product.

That means the source system decides what is in scope, returns the identifiers and lineage, exposes supportability, and tells the consumer whether the answer is complete enough to use.

The consuming workflow should preserve that evidence. It should not infer missing membership. It should not silently launch a partial cohort. It should not turn a bounded candidate list into a global universe claim.

This is less glamorous than adding another button.

But it is the kind of design that makes a front-office platform easier to trust. The workflow can stay focused on orchestration and evidence, while the source owner remains accountable for the facts that decide who is in scope.
