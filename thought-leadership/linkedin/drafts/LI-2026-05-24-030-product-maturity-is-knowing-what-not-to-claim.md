Product maturity is not only about adding capability.

It is also about knowing what not to claim.

In wealth platforms, one implemented path can sit next to many adjacent concepts: portfolio universe discovery, relationship householding, workflow orchestration, client communication, approvals, execution, settlement, and reconciliation.

It is tempting to describe the feature in the language of the broader journey.

But that can create a dangerous ambiguity.

A bounded candidate source is not the same as global universe ownership. A source-backed workflow is not the same as external task orchestration. Review evidence is not the same as trade approval. An internal handoff is not the same as OMS execution.

The stronger product habit is to write the boundary down.

What is supported?
What is source-owned?
What fails closed?
What is explicitly outside the claim?

This kind of precision may look conservative, but it builds trust with the people who have to operate, support, audit, and extend the platform.

A roadmap can still be ambitious.

The current product truth should be exact.
