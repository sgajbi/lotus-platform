A proposal memo should not be treated as document generation.

In advisory platforms, the memo is closer to an evidence product.

It should carry the recommendation context, suitability posture, alternatives considered, risk and impact view, approval state, report package references, and the lineage needed to replay how the answer was formed.

That matters because different users read the memo for different reasons.

An advisor needs a client-conversation anchor.
A compliance reviewer needs the control evidence.
Operations needs to know what can move next.
Audit needs a record that survives the handoff.
Product teams need to know exactly what is supported and what remains outside the claim.

If the memo is only a rendered artifact, too much business truth sits somewhere else.

If it is an evidence product, the workflow becomes easier to trust, support, and extend.

The strongest advisory experiences are not just well-written.

They are source-backed, reviewable, replayable, and clear about their boundaries.
