Policy evidence is most useful when it also explains what it does not prove.

In advisory workflows, a policy check can easily be misunderstood as a final approval. A queue can look like sign-off. A completed evaluation can be read as permission to publish.

Those are different control points.

Good platform design keeps them separate.

The evaluation should show the policy version, source evidence, rule outcomes, gaps, disclosures, and review posture. It should be replayable. It should be visible to the teams that need to support, review, and explain it.

But it should not quietly become waiver authority.
It should not imply completed sign-off.
It should not turn into client-ready communication just because the evidence is complete.

That boundary matters.

It protects users from over-reading the workflow, helps operations support the platform, and gives compliance teams clearer evidence to challenge.

In wealth technology, maturity is not only proving that a control passed.

It is also making clear what still requires human review, approval, and accountable release.
