A demo is strongest when it shows what the workflow can prove and where it stops.

In advisory platforms, it is easy for a proof pack to sound like approval. A generated memo can
sound like client communication. A policy result can sound like sign-off. A handoff can sound like
execution.

Those are different controls.

Good demo evidence keeps the distinction visible.

It should show the scenario, source evidence, review posture, blocked claims, audit trail, and
supportability state. It should also say clearly when client-ready publication, external
communication, or order execution is not part of the supported path.

That does not weaken the story. It makes it more credible.

Wealth platforms are bought and operated by people who need to trust the boundary as much as the
feature. A repeatable demo should therefore prove both: what the platform can do, and what it will
not overclaim.

The best proof is not the smoothest screen.

It is evidence that survives review.
