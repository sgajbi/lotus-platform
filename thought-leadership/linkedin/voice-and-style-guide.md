# Voice And Style Guide

The writing should sound like a senior practitioner reflecting on real wealth-platform work.

## Voice

Use a voice that is:

1. direct,
2. practical,
3. domain-aware,
4. calm,
5. specific,
6. credible without being self-promotional.

The author can say "I" when describing a lesson or perspective. Avoid sounding like a company
announcement.

## Structure Patterns

Use one of these structures:

1. Problem, insight, principle.
2. Common misconception, better framing, example.
3. Field lesson, implication, design rule.
4. Domain concept, why it matters, what good looks like.
5. Leadership lesson, product impact, practical takeaway.

## Length

Target 120-220 words for most posts.

Shorter posts are acceptable when the point is sharp. Longer posts should earn their length through
specificity.

## Public Safety Guardrails

Do not include:

1. confidential employer information,
2. client names,
3. production incident details that could identify an organization,
4. internal bank architecture,
5. screenshots or data not approved for public use,
6. claims that Lotus has features not implemented or proved,
7. investment advice,
8. regulatory advice.

## Employer-Safe Framing

The author works in a major bank, so posts must not invite readers to infer that an observation is
about the author's employer, team, platform, users, incidents, or internal priorities.

Use these rules:

1. never mention the employer unless the user explicitly asks and the reference is already public,
2. frame points as industry-wide design principles, not insider commentary,
3. prefer constructive "what good platforms need" language over criticism of weak platforms,
4. avoid wording such as "many banks fail" or "most systems are broken",
5. avoid active-work hints, rollout details, locations, team structures, internal architecture,
   production incidents, vendor names, or user behavior,
6. keep Lotus separate from employer work and never imply the employer uses Lotus,
7. use "wealth technology", "portfolio analytics", or "banking platforms" as general domains,
8. when in doubt, soften the post to a positive design principle or do not draft it.

## Style Checklist

Before calling a draft ready, check:

1. Does it make one clear point?
2. Is the point rooted in real domain or delivery experience?
3. Would a senior banking technology reader find it credible?
4. Does it avoid confidential or traceable details?
5. Does it avoid direct Lotus marketing?
6. Does it sound human rather than generated?
7. Does it create a reason for the right audience to follow the author?
8. Could a reader reasonably infer this is about the author's employer? If yes, rewrite it.

## Tone Examples

Prefer:

> A portfolio dashboard is only useful if the user trusts the number, understands the source, and
> knows what to do when the number looks wrong.

Avoid:

> In today's fast-paced digital world, wealth platforms must leverage innovation to transform the
> client journey.
