# Commercial Model and GTM

## Purpose

This page reframes the older core-level GTM material into current Lotus ecosystem language.

It is not a public price list. It is a platform-level commercial framing page that explains how
Lotus can be packaged and discussed consistently.

## What Lotus can package

### Core platform offering

The baseline offer is the Lotus ecosystem operating model:

- governed product surface
- experience API layer
- domain-authoritative services
- platform automation, standards, and validation

### Expandable capability layers

Lotus can grow through bounded capability expansion across:

- portfolio and transaction authority
- performance analytics
- risk analytics
- advisory workflows
- management workflows
- reporting and evidence
- governed AI-backed capabilities

### Platform and delivery services

Platform value can also be expressed through:

- environment setup and migration support
- integration mapping and rollout planning
- QA and validation hardening
- operational readiness and supportability guidance
- performance and capacity tuning

## Commercial framing

The clean Lotus message is:

- product value comes from the ecosystem
- commercial value improves when the platform layer reduces delivery and operating cost
- adoption can start narrow and expand without redesigning the whole estate

## Packaging options

Packaging can be expressed through a mix of:

- platform and capability subscription
- deployment-tier uplift
- support and SLA tiers
- professional services for integration, migration, and rollout

The exact pricing model should remain deal-specific rather than being hardcoded into the engineering
wiki.

## Go-to-market posture

### Enterprise-led motion

Best for:

- private banks
- wealth managers
- institutions modernizing legacy estates

Typical emphasis:

- governance
- integration control
- supportability
- phased rollout without full-suite replacement

### Platform and fintech motion

Best for:

- digital wealth platforms
- fintechs embedding investment workflows

Typical emphasis:

- API-first adoption
- modular capability packaging
- faster product delivery
- scalable backend foundations

## Why Lotus is commercially credible

Lotus can be positioned credibly because the engineering system already reflects:

- explicit ownership boundaries
- repeatable validation lanes
- environment-scoped service governance
- operational evidence and QA automation
- modular capability separation instead of vague platform rhetoric

## Commercial cautions

- do not describe `lotus-platform` as the product surface itself
- do not collapse all domain services into one generic “engine” message
- do not reuse old PAS-era pricing or packaging language without translating it into current Lotus
  architecture
- do not publish hard numeric price points in repo documentation
